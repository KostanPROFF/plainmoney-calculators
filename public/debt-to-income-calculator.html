What is a good debt-to-income ratio?

36% or less is considered healthy and qualifies for the best rates. 37% to 43% is acceptable for most mortgages, and 43% is the traditional limit for a qualified mortgage. 44% to 49% is possible with some programs, such as FHA loans with strong credit or reserves. 50% and above makes most loans hard to get.

What counts as debt

Count required monthly payments: housing, car loans, student loans, minimum card payments, personal loans, alimony and child support. Utilities, groceries, phone bills and insurance premiums are not included. Use income before taxes.

Worked examples

Example: $6,000 monthly income

Rent is $1,600, a car loan is $400, student loans are $250 and card minimums are $100, for $2,350 in monthly debt. Dividing by $6,000 gives a DTI of 39.2%. The housing ratio is 26.7%, inside the 28% guideline, but total debt is above 36%. Paying off the $400 car loan would bring the DTI down to 32.5%.

Frequently asked questions

What DTI do I need for a mortgage?

Most conventional loans look for 36% or less and accept up to about 45% with strong credit. FHA loans can allow up to 50% in some cases. Lower is always better for approval and rates.

Does DTI affect my credit score?

No. Credit scores do not use income, so DTI is not part of your score. Lenders calculate it separately when you apply. Credit card utilization, which compares balances to limits, does affect your score.

Should I use gross or net income?

Use gross income, before taxes and deductions. That is what lenders use. If you are self-employed, lenders usually average your net business income from the last two tax returns.

How can I lower my DTI quickly?

Pay off small loans entirely, since that removes a whole payment. Paying down credit cards lowers the minimum payment. Adding income, such as a co-borrower's salary, also lowers the ratio.
