Interest rate vs. APR

The interest rate sets your payment. The APR adds the origination fee, which lenders usually subtract from the money you receive, so you pay interest on cash you never get. When comparing offers, compare APRs. A loan at 11% with a 6% fee can cost more than one at 13% with no fee.

Typical rates

Personal loan APRs in the US range from about 7% for excellent credit to 36% for fair or poor credit. Credit unions often charge less and rarely add origination fees.

Worked examples

Example: comparing two $10,000 offers

Offer A has an 11% rate and a 6% origination fee over 36 months. The payment is $327.39 and interest totals about $1,790, but the $600 fee is taken out up front, so you receive $9,400. The true APR is about 15.3%.

Offer B has a 13% rate with no fee. The payment is $336.94 and interest is about $2,130, but you receive the full $10,000, so the APR is 13%. Offer B costs less overall even though its rate looks higher.

Frequently asked questions

What is an origination fee?

It is a one-time fee, usually 1% to 10% of the loan, that many online lenders charge. It is normally subtracted from the money you receive, so you repay the full loan amount while getting less cash.

What is the difference between interest rate and APR?

The interest rate determines your monthly payment. The APR also includes fees, so it shows the real yearly cost of borrowing. Use the APR to compare offers from different lenders.

How much should I borrow if there is a fee?

If you need a specific amount in hand, divide it by one minus the fee rate. To receive $10,000 with a 6% fee, you would borrow about $10,640.

Does paying off a personal loan early save money?

Yes, because interest is charged on the remaining balance. Most personal lenders do not charge prepayment penalties, but check your loan agreement first.
