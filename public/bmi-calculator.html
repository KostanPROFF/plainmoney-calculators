How BMI is calculated

BMI is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in meters squared. In US units the formula is BMI = 703 × weight (lb) / height (in)2. A person 5 feet 9 inches tall (69 inches) who weighs 170 pounds has a BMI of 703 × 170 / 4,761 = 25.1.

What BMI does not show

BMI is a quick screening number, not a diagnosis. It does not separate muscle from fat, so athletes can score as overweight, and it does not show where fat is stored. Waist size, blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol give a fuller picture. These ranges are for adults 20 and older; children and teens use BMI-for-age percentiles.

Worked examples

Example 1: 5 feet 4 inches and 145 pounds

Height is 64 inches, so BMI is 703 × 145 / 642 = 24.9, at the top of the healthy range. For this height, a healthy weight runs from about 108 to 145 pounds.

Example 2: 6 feet and 220 pounds

At 72 inches, BMI is 703 × 220 / 722 = 29.8, which falls in the overweight range. The healthy range for this height is about 136 to 184 pounds, so reaching it means losing about 37 pounds.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

For adults, the CDC defines a BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 as a healthy weight. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25 to 29.9 is overweight, and 30 or higher is obesity.

Is BMI accurate?

BMI is a useful screening tool for groups of people, but it can mislead for individuals. It cannot tell muscle from fat, so muscular people may show a high BMI, and older adults with little muscle may show a normal BMI with excess fat.

Is BMI different for men and women?

No. Adults use the same formula and the same categories regardless of sex. Women tend to carry more body fat than men at the same BMI, which is one of the limits of the measure.

Can children use this calculator?

No. For children and teens aged 2 to 19, BMI is compared with others of the same age and sex using growth charts, so the adult categories do not apply.
