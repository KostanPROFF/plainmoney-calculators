What comes out of your paycheck

Federal income tax is calculated on your salary minus pre-tax deductions and the 2026 standard deduction ($16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly, $24,150 head of household), using the 2026 brackets from 10% to 37%.

FICA is 6.2% for Social Security on wages up to $184,500 in 2026, plus 1.45% for Medicare on all wages and an extra 0.9% above $200,000 ($250,000 for joint filers).

State income tax varies. Nine states have no wage income tax: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming. Flat-tax states use their single rate. For states with graduated brackets this version uses an approximate effective rate that you can override. Local and city taxes are not included.

Worked examples

Example: $60,000 salary paid every two weeks

A single filer contributes 5% ($3,000 a year) to a traditional 401(k). Federal taxable income is $60,000 minus $3,000 minus the $16,100 standard deduction, or $40,900, which gives about $4,660 in federal income tax. Social Security takes $3,720 and Medicare $870. In Texas, with no state income tax, take-home pay is about $47,750 a year, or $1,836.54 per paycheck.

The same salary in Illinois, at a flat 4.95%, adds about $2,820 in state tax, so each paycheck drops to about $1,728.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my paycheck different from this estimate?

Your employer withholds based on your Form W-4, which may differ from your actual tax. Health, dental and other benefit deductions, local taxes and state disability insurance can also reduce your check.

What is the 2026 standard deduction?

It is $16,100 for single filers, $32,200 for married couples filing jointly and $24,150 for heads of household. It is subtracted from your income before federal tax brackets apply.

Do 401(k) contributions reduce my taxes?

Traditional 401(k) contributions reduce federal and most state income tax, but not Social Security or Medicare tax. Roth 401(k) contributions are taxed now and come out tax-free in retirement.

What is the difference between marginal and effective tax rate?

Your marginal rate is the rate on your last dollar of income. Your effective rate is total tax divided by total income, which is always lower because the first dollars are taxed at lower rates.

Which states have no income tax?

Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming do not tax wages. Washington taxes some capital gains.
