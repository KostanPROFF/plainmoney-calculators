2026 401(k) contribution limits

You can defer up to $24,500 of salary in 2026. Workers aged 50 and older can add an $8,000 catch-up contribution, and those aged 60 to 63 get a higher catch-up of $11,250. Employer contributions do not count toward your personal limit. This calculator caps your own contributions at these limits and holds them flat in later years.

Always get the full match

An employer match is an instant return on your money. If your plan matches 50% up to 6% of pay, contributing at least 6% is the easiest raise you will ever get.

Worked examples

Example: starting at 35 with $25,000 saved

You earn $60,000, get 3% raises, contribute 10% and your employer matches 50% up to 6% of pay. With a 7% annual return, your balance at 65 could reach about $1.24 million. Your own contributions total about $285,500 and your employer adds about $85,600; the rest is investment growth.

Contributing only 3% instead gives about $552,500 at 65, because both your deposits and the match are smaller. Contributing at least enough to get the full match is the single biggest step.

Frequently asked questions

How much can I contribute to a 401(k) in 2026?

The employee limit is $24,500. Workers aged 50 and older can add $8,000, and those aged 60 to 63 can add $11,250 instead. Employer contributions do not count toward your personal limit.

What return should I assume?

Many planners use 5% to 7% a year for a diversified stock and bond portfolio before inflation. Returns vary a lot from year to year, so it helps to test a lower rate as well.

What does an employer match mean?

Your employer adds money based on what you contribute. A 50% match up to 6% of pay means that if you put in 6%, your employer adds 3%. Contributing less than the match limit leaves free money on the table.

Should I choose a traditional or Roth 401(k)?

Traditional contributions lower your taxes now and are taxed when you withdraw. Roth contributions are taxed now and are tax-free in retirement. If you expect a higher tax rate later, Roth often makes sense.

What does today's dollars mean?

It adjusts the future balance for 2.5% yearly inflation, showing what that amount would buy at today's prices.
